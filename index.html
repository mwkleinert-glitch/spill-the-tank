A low advertised prebuy price is not always what you pay in January.

Any propane company can advertise a low locked-in price during contract season. Once that season ends, will-call customers pay a different price, often much higher. The advertised price is usually a starting price. It may not include sales tax, delivery or service fees, environmental or hazmat fees, or tank rental and lease fees.

Locked-in / prebuy vs. will-call vs. budget

Locked-in or prebuy prices are rates you agree to in advance, often in spring or summer, for a set volume or period. Will-call prices are what you pay when you call for a delivery with no prior agreement. Budget plans typically spread estimated annual costs into equal monthly payments, with a true-up at the end.

When a locked-in or prebuy contract ends mid-winter, remaining deliveries usually switch to the current will-call rate, which is frequently higher. The advertised locked-in price is almost always the base per-gallon rate only.

Common add-ons to ask about

  • Sales tax
  • Delivery or service fees
  • Environmental / hazmat fees
  • Tank rental or lease fees
  • Minimum-delivery charges
  • After-hours or emergency delivery fees
  • Leak check fees
  • Tank removal or pickup fees

These can add to the total. Ask for the all-in price or a full fee schedule.

Questions to ask before you sign

  • Is this price locked for the full season or a specific volume? What happens if you need more?
  • What is the current will-call price, and how often does it change?
  • Which fees are included in the quoted price? Which are extra?
  • Is the tank owned or leased? What does the lease cost?
  • Are there minimum-delivery, after-hours, or leak-check charges?
  • What does it cost to switch suppliers or remove the tank later?
  • Can you see a recent sample bill that shows every line item?

This is general information. Pricing varies by company and place. Confirm the details with your supplier.
